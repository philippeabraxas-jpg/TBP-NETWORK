**Last verified**: 2026-09-29

Legend: ✅ Covered · 🔴 Real gap (needs code; the row links the issue that tracks it) · ⚪ Not TBP's concern (the row says why and, for a deployment, how to close it). A combined status splits one control between what TBP covers and what stays with the deployer. See the [catalogue README](README.md).

## The 5 criteria vs. TBP

| Criterion | Content | Status | Detail |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Security** (mandatory) | Protection against unauthorized access, disclosure, damage | ✅ | Same foundation as [#152](152-iso-iec-27001-27002.md)/[#153](153-nist-csf-800-53.md): OPA/`AgentRegistry`/`QuorumGate`/HSM/mTLS. |
| **Availability** | Uptime, resilience, disaster recovery, capacity | ⚪ deliberate tradeoff | TBP's fail-closed doctrine DELIBERATELY prioritizes safety over availability — an unreachable OPA, an unverified bundle, an unknown identity all mean total refusal, never a permissive degraded mode. A SOC 2 audit focused on availability must document this as an assumed design tradeoff, not a defect to fix. **To close:** document the tradeoff in the system description, and run redundant cells where you have availability commitments. |
| **Processing Integrity** | Accuracy, completeness, timeliness of processing | ✅ | Solid: the `writeLeaf` doctrine ("an allow without proof reverts to a refusal") guarantees a decision is ALWAYS either complete (leaf + token) or fully refused — never a partial state. An atomicity guarantee, exactly what this criterion asks for. |
| **Confidentiality** | Protection of non-public business information | ✅ | Hash-only leaf doctrine (§6.2), already cited for EU AI Act Art. 12 ([#146](146-eu-ai-act.md)) and ISO 27001 DLP ([#152](152-iso-iec-27001-27002.md)). |
| **Privacy** | Collection/use/storage/disclosure/disposal of personal data | ✅ minimisation · ⚪ consent and lifecycle | Hash-only protects against LEAKING any PII that would transit through TBP — but TBP has no concept of consent, right-to-erasure, or personal-data lifecycle (see [GDPR, #155](155-gdpr.md) for full treatment). **To close:** the data controller handles consent and personal-data lifecycle; TBP's part is that no personal content reaches its audit trail. |

---

## Summary

**The most important point in this catalog is a nuance, not a status**: TBP's Availability-vs-Security tradeoff must be written down explicitly in any documentation aimed at a SOC 2 auditor — without that, an audit could misread fail-closed as a resilience shortfall rather than a deliberate, documented security choice (TBP doctrine §1).

**Processing Integrity is an unexpectedly strong, direct match** — no prior framework in this series had as direct an equivalent; a good addition to TBP's evidence file.
