# ISO/IEC 23894:2023 (AI risk management, 8 risk sources)

**Status: Partial**
**Source**: [issue #150](https://github.com/philippeabraxas-jpg/TBP-NETWORK/issues/150)
**Reference**: ISO/IEC 23894:2023 — AI risk-management guidance, applying ISO 31000:2018's general risk-management methodology to the AI context. A GUIDANCE document, no "shall" requirements or third-party audit structure — no numbered controls to check off.
**Last verified**: 2026-09-28

## Scope note

The generic process (establish context, identify, analyze, evaluate, treat, monitor, review, record, report) leads to the SAME conclusion already reached for the [NIST AI RMF (#147)](147-nist-ai-rmf.md): TBP is a risk-*treatment* component (`Manage`/`Treat`), not the risk-management process itself. Rather than repeating that, this catalog focuses on the standard's **8 AI-specific risk sources**, the genuinely new content relative to #147.

Legend: ✅ Covered/strong technical evidence · 🟡 Partial · 🔴 Real gap · ⚪ Out of TBP's scope

## The 8 AI risk sources vs. TBP

| Risk source | Status | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **Automation** | 🟡 | OPA's default-deny and class-W quorum (§7.5) are a direct answer to uncontrolled automation risk — an automated action never executes without passing this filter. |
| **Explainability** | 🟡 | Machine-readable `Reason` codes give DECISION-level explainability (why the policy ruled this way) — not explainability of the model's own reasoning, out of reach. |
| **Complexity** | 🟡 | TBP REDUCES decision complexity to a bounded, auditable chain (§5.1) — a partial answer, not an elimination of the underlying model's complexity. |
| **Data quality** | ⚪ | Training data — out of scope, same as everywhere else in this series (LLM02/05 in [#143](143-owasp-llm-top10.md), Art. 10 in [#146](146-eu-ai-act.md)). |
| **Drift** | 🟡 | The OPA bundle revision-check (§92.A5) detects POLICY drift; it never detects the MODEL's own behavioral drift — an important distinction not to blur. |
| **Lifecycle** | 🟡 | Same remark as ISO 42001 A.6 ([#148](148-iso-iec-42001.md)): TBP's own development lifecycle is solid and demonstrated (non-vacuous test discipline, CI, `selftest.sh`); the authorized model/agent's lifecycle stays out of reach. |
| **Security** | ✅ | TBP's strongest point on this list: non-extractable HSM (#114), mTLS (#124), anti-replay, generalized fail-closed — literally the project's reason for existing. |
| **Privacy** | ✅ | Hash-only leaf doctrine (§6.2) — the same mechanism already cited for EU AI Act Art. 12 ([#146](146-eu-ai-act.md)) and detailed fully in [GDPR (#155)](155-gdpr.md). |

## Summary

**Same pattern confirmed as in [#147](147-nist-ai-rmf.md)**: TBP is strong on "technical" risk sources (security, privacy), medium on those touching its own decision chain (automation, explainability, complexity), and absent on those touching the model/training data (data quality, model drift, model lifecycle).

**Distinction to document explicitly**: TBP detects drift in THE POLICY (OPA bundle), never in THE MODEL — two notions of "drift" that must never be conflated in compliance documentation.

**No new SkillRegistry overlap here** — consistent, this standard doesn't address third-party/installable components.
