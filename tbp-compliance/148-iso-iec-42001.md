# ISO/IEC 42001:2023 (AIMS, Annex A — 9 categories)

**Status: Full**
**Source**: [issue #148](https://github.com/philippeabraxas-jpg/TBP-NETWORK/issues/148) · fix: [issue #165 / PR #166](https://github.com/philippeabraxas-jpg/TBP-NETWORK/issues/165) (SkillRegistry)
**Reference**: ISO/IEC 42001:2023 — AI Management System (AIMS), structured like other ISO management standards (27001, 9001): a core process body + a 38-control Annex A grouped into 9 categories (A.2–A.10). An organization selects applicable controls and justifies exclusions in a Statement of Applicability (SoA) — TBP can never *be* the certification itself, only technical evidence for some categories.
**Last verified**: 2026-09-29

Legend: ✅ Covered · 🔴 Real gap (needs code; the row links the issue that tracks it) · ⚪ Not TBP's concern (the row says why and, for a deployment, how to close it). A combined status splits one control between what TBP covers and what stays with the deployer. See the [catalogue README](README.md).

## The 9 categories vs. TBP

| Category | Content | Status | Detail |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A.2** — AI policy | AIMS policy foundation | ⚪ | Organizational document, not a software feature. |
| **A.3** — Internal organization, roles | Internal roles and responsibilities | ⚪ | Organizational. |
| **A.4** — Resources | Resources allocated to the AIMS | ⚪ | Organizational (TBP can be CITED as a tool/resource, but the requirement is about allocation, not the tool). |
| **A.5** — Impact assessment | Assess AI system impact before/during use | ⚪ | TBP CONSUMES the result of an impact assessment (an action's F/I/W class, resolved by `AgentRegistry`, #125) — it never performs the assessment itself. **To close:** perform the impact assessment and set each agent's class in the registry from its result. |
| **A.6** — AI system lifecycle | Design, development, testing, deployment | ✅ TBP's lifecycle · ⚪ the agent's | Two distinct readings: TBP's OWN development lifecycle is solid and already exercised in this repo (fail-closed doctrine, non-vacuous test discipline, `selftest.sh`, CI) — a good citable example; the lifecycle of the MODEL/AGENT that TBP authorizes stays out of reach. **To close (the agent's lifecycle):** apply your own AI lifecycle process to the model and agent. |
| **A.7** — Data governance | Quality/governance of data used by the AI | ⚪ | Out of scope for TRAINING data (as elsewhere in this series); narrowly ✅ for the data TBP produces ITSELF (hash-only leaves, §6.2 — minimal but real governance of its own audit data). **To close:** govern training data in your data-governance process; TBP's own audit data is hash-only (§6.2). |
| **A.8** — Stakeholder transparency | Clear information on how the AI system operates | ⚪ | Same remark as EU AI Act Art. 13 ([#146](146-eu-ai-act.md)): `Reason` codes and the audit chain are usable raw material, not the transparency deliverable itself. **To close:** as EU AI Act Art. 13 ([#146](146-eu-ai-act.md)). |
| **A.9** — Responsible use of AI systems | Restrictions and responsible-use policies | ✅ | The most actionable category: OPA's default-deny plus the F/I/W classes are exactly the technical ENFORCEMENT mechanism for a responsible-use policy, once that policy is written in Rego. The rule packs (`policies/rego/pack_*.rego`) are named, tested examples of exactly this: a responsible-use policy written in Rego and enforced by default-deny. |
| **A.10** — Third-party relationships (suppliers, customers) | Managing relationships with third-party AI component suppliers | ✅ | **Closed** — `SkillRegistry` (#165, PR #166) is exactly the mechanism requested here, under a "supplier management" angle: `SkillRecord.Provenance` documents the source, `Scope` bounds what a third-party skill can target, and no admission happens without out-of-band operator provisioning. |

---

## Summary

**Most actionable category**: A.9 (responsible use) — OPA's default-deny has direct, immediately demonstrable value here.

**Closed since the original catalog**: A.10, by `SkillRegistry` (#165, PR #166) — the fifth independent framework (after [#142](142-owasp-agentic-skills-top10.md), [#143](143-owasp-llm-top10.md), [#144](144-csa-maestro.md), [#145](145-mitre-atlas.md)) that pointed at this exact gap.

**Compared to the RMF ([#147](147-nist-ai-rmf.md))**: ISO 42001 is more granular (9 categories vs. 4 functions) but the structural conclusion is identical — most categories are organizational, and TBP only has technical traction on a minority of them (A.5, A.6 partially, A.9, now A.10).
